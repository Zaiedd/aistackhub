A freelancer I know spent an entire Sunday reconciling four months of Stripe payouts against her bank feed, categorizing each line by hand. She then paid $49/month for a tool whose headline feature was that it would do exactly that automatically. The next month, she still checked every single line — because when the money is yours, you don't trust a model you can't see.

That tension is the whole story of "AI bookkeeping" for a small business. The automation is real. The time savings are real. But the honest question isn't "which AI is smartest" — it's "do I need a separate AI layer at all, or does my accounting software already do enough of this?"

## First, a reality check on what "AI" means here

In consumer tools, "AI" often means a chatbot. In bookkeeping, it almost always means one of three unglamorous things:

- **Transaction categorization** — the tool reads a merchant name and amount and guesses a category (Software, Meals, Contractors), learning from your corrections over time.
- **Reconciliation** — matching bank-feed lines against invoices and receipts, flagging the ones it can't pair.
- **Document extraction** — pulling totals, line items, and tax off a PDF invoice or a photographed receipt.

That's genuinely useful work — it's the repetitive part of bookkeeping. But notice what it is not: it is not a qualified accountant reviewing your books, and it does not catch a category that's consistently wrong if you keep confirming it. The value of the "AI" is entirely proportional to how much of this repetitive work you actually have, and how willing you are to review its guesses.

For a solopreneur with 30 transactions a month, a mainstream tool's built-in automation is usually enough. For a business running 400 invoices through accounts payable, a dedicated extraction layer starts to earn its keep.

## The real decision: add-on, built-in, or switch

Once you strip away the marketing, most small businesses land in one of three situations.

**1. You already run QuickBooks or Xero.** The cheapest, lowest-risk move is usually to turn on the automation already sitting in your plan before paying for anything extra. Xero's bank matching and QuickBooks' categorization have improved substantially, and for many businesses that's the entire "AI" feature set they needed. A separate AI tool that layers on top (the approach taken by services like Finlens, Booke AI, and Digits) adds confidence scores and a review queue — valuable when you want to see *only* the uncertain transactions, less so when you have few transactions to begin with.

**2. You want full-service, hands-off finance.** Products like Zeni and Botkeeper are closer to managed bookkeeping with software than to pure software. That's a legitimate trade for a funded startup that wants bill pay, reporting, and an advisor without hiring — but it's priced accordingly (Zeni's public pricing starts around $494–$549/month depending on billing), so it's the wrong call for a bootstrapped one-person shop.

**3. Your bottleneck is invoices or expenses specifically.** If accounts payable is the pain, tools like Vic.ai target high invoice volume with deep extraction. If it's expenses, Rydoo's audit layer flags non-compliant or duplicate claims (its Essentials plan is listed from around $9/user/month). These solve a narrow problem well, so buy them for that problem, not as a general ledger.

## Where the honest cost comparison lands

Prices move constantly, so treat these as rough starting points and check the vendor's current page before you commit — but the shape of the market is stable:

- **Free or near-free tier:** Wave is still the reference point for freelancers who want invoicing and expense tracking without a monthly fee, and Zoho Books' entry tier sits in the low-tens-of-dollars range. If your needs are simple, this is where to start.
- **Mainstream paid tier:** Xero and QuickBooks Online start in the roughly $15–$40/month range. You're paying for integrations, reporting, and better automation — not for a separate "AI" product.
- **AI-first add-on or managed service:** This is where pricing jumps to the hundreds per month. Only worth it when the hours it saves are worth more than the fee — which, for most small businesses, means you've already hit the limits of tier one or two.

## The trap worth naming

The single most common mistake is paying for an AI layer to fix a bookkeeping habit problem. If you're behind on data entry, no categorization model fixes that — you still have to connect the bank feed and log the receipts. The AI removes categorization drudgery, not procrastination. Get the feed connected and the basics automated in a $15–$40 tool first; a dedicated AI layer only makes sense once you're demonstrably drowning in volume, not before.
