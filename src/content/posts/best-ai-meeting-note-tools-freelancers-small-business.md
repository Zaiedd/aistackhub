It’s 4:15 PM. You just finished the third client discovery call of the day. Your notebook has three half-sentences and a coffee stain. The client said something important about timeline and budget around minute 27 and you already forgot the exact wording. You need a clean summary, action items, and a follow-up email before the next call starts in twelve minutes.

That is the real job these tools are supposed to do in 2026. I tested the three that keep showing up for freelancers and small teams — Fathom, Fireflies.ai, and Otter.ai — by running the same set of client and internal calls through each one. I looked at three things only: how usable the free plan actually is, how clean the summary is, and whether the tool stays out of the way or starts feeling like another person in the meeting.

## What freelancers and tiny teams actually need

You do not need Gong-level conversation intelligence. You need:

- A reliable transcript and summary after the call ends
- Action items you can paste straight into a task list or email
- Something that does not eat half your billable hour in setup or cost
- Preferably a free or very cheap tier that still works for 8–12 meetings a week

Anything that requires a sales call, a minimum of five seats, or a visible bot that makes clients ask “who is that?” is already the wrong tool for most solo operators.

## Fathom — the one that stays free the longest

Fathom’s free plan is still the most generous in the category as of September 2026. Unlimited recordings and transcriptions, instant AI summaries, clips, and search across your calls. The paid Premium plan sits around $16–20 per user per month (annual vs monthly). Team plans start lower per seat once you hit two users.

On my test calls the summaries were short, readable, and usually pulled the right action items. The interface is clean. You can choose bot or bot-free capture on supported platforms. For a freelancer who just wants the notes without another monthly bill, Fathom is the default starting point. The main limit on free is the number of advanced AI summaries (around five per month on the free tier in current plans); after that you still get the recording and basic transcript.

## Fireflies.ai — when you need CRM and search across many calls

Fireflies is stronger once the volume goes up or you start pushing notes into HubSpot or Salesforce. Free plan gives unlimited transcription with limited storage (roughly 400 minutes for the team in recent 2026 pricing). Pro is about $10 per seat per month billed annually ($18 monthly). Business lands around $19 annual / $29 monthly.

The summaries are solid and the search across past meetings is better than Fathom’s free tier. CRM push is the feature that actually matters for anyone running a small sales or client pipeline. The trade-off is the bot that joins the call by default and the slightly more complex interface. For a solo consultant with light volume, it is more tool than you need. For a two- or three-person team that lives in meetings, it starts to earn its keep.

## Otter.ai — still useful if you want live captions

Otter remains the strongest option if you actually want to read the transcript while the meeting is happening. Free plan is limited to 300 minutes per month with a 30-minute cap per conversation. Pro is roughly $8.33 per user per month on annual billing ($16.99 monthly) for 1,200 minutes. Business sits higher.

Accuracy on clean audio is good. The live view is the differentiator. For most freelancers who only need the notes after the call, the free-tier limits make it less practical than Fathom. If your work involves long workshops or you like following along in real time, Otter still has a place.

## A realistic stack for 2026

Most freelancers and two-person shops do not need three tools. Start with Fathom free. If you hit the advanced-summary limit often or need CRM push, move the higher-volume calls to Fireflies Pro. Keep Otter only if live transcription is a real requirement.

The biggest time save is not the perfect summary. It is never having to reconstruct the call from memory at 10 PM. All three tools deliver that. The difference is how much you pay and how much friction the bot and the free-tier limits add.
