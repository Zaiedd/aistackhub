It's 9:40 PM and the application is due at midnight. You have a solid resume, a job description that actually matches your experience, and a blank cover letter staring back at you. The old advice was to spend two hours crafting a personal letter. The 2026 reality is that most people will use AI for the first draft — and the quality gap between tools is larger than the marketing suggests.

I took one real candidate profile and one real mid-level job posting and ran them through the tools that keep showing up in 2026 roundups. I looked at three things: how specific the output was to the job description, whether it invented achievements, and how much editing was still required before I would hit submit. Generic praise and inflated numbers got cut immediately.

## What actually matters for cover letters and CVs

The job of these tools is not to write the perfect letter for you. It is to get you from a blank page to a strong first draft faster, without sounding like every other AI-generated application.

Three criteria matter:

1. **Job-specific tailoring** — does the output reference the actual posting, or just swap in the company name?
2. **Honesty constraint** — does it invent metrics or responsibilities you never claimed?
3. **Edit distance** — how much do you still have to rewrite before it sounds like a human who wants the job?

Everything else (pretty templates, unlimited downloads) is secondary.

## Claude and ChatGPT — still the strongest free starting point for writing

When the goal is the quality of the prose itself, the general models still win. I fed Claude and ChatGPT the same resume bullets and the same job description. Claude produced the more natural opening and the better quantified achievement framing. ChatGPT was faster at generating three different versions so I could pick the strongest direction.

Both will invent details if you let them. The fix is simple and non-negotiable: paste only the experience you actually have, and add the instruction "Do not invent metrics or responsibilities. Only use what I provided." Even then, read every sentence. Recruiters have seen the generic AI patterns and they notice.

As of September 2026 both free tiers are usable for occasional applications. Paid plans ($20/month range) mainly buy higher limits and better models when you are applying to many roles in a short window.

## Dedicated builders — Teal, Rezi, Kickresume

When you need the full application workflow (resume + cover letter + tracking), dedicated tools start to make sense.

**Teal** remains one of the strongest free-to-start options. The free plan includes an AI resume builder, basic tailoring, and a job tracker. Cover letter generation sits inside the same workspace, so the letter and the resume stay consistent. Paid Teal+ (roughly $29/month or weekly options) unlocks higher limits. It is the tool I would give someone who is applying to many roles and needs everything in one place.

**Rezi** is the ATS-first choice. Its scoring and keyword targeting are more aggressive than most competitors. The free plan is limited; Pro sits around $29/month (or a lifetime option that appears periodically). If your main fear is the applicant tracking system, Rezi is built for that job.

**Kickresume** wins on design and template variety. AI writing quality is solid, and the matching resume + letter templates look polished. Pricing is more accessible for many users (often starting lower when billed yearly). It is a better fit when the role is design-forward or the visual presentation matters as much as the keywords.

Jobscan is useful as a companion for keyword matching against a specific posting, but it is not primarily a writer. Use it to check the finished letter, not to generate it.

## A realistic workflow that does not waste hours

1. Paste your real experience and the job description into Claude or ChatGPT with a strict "no invention" instruction. Generate the first draft of the cover letter.
2. Move the strongest version into Teal or Kickresume if you need a clean template and matching resume.
3. Run the final pair through a keyword checker (Jobscan or the built-in score in Rezi/Teal) and fix only the genuine gaps.
4. Edit until it sounds like you. The last pass is always human.

Most people do not need a $30/month tool for one or two applications a month. The free general models plus careful editing are enough. The dedicated platforms earn their keep when volume is high or ATS rejection is the actual bottleneck.

## What still fails

Every tool I tested produced at least one generic phrase or slightly inflated claim that needed cutting. AI is excellent at structure and first-draft speed. It is still weak at genuine specificity and at knowing which of your experiences actually matter for this exact role. That judgment stays with you.
