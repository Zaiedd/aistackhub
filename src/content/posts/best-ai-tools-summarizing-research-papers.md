## What we tested

We ran the same five papers (mixed fields: CS, biology, economics) through each tool and checked three things: did it preserve the actual findings, did it invent anything, and how usable was the output for someone about to cite the paper.

- **Tool B** — best when you need section-by-section breakdowns (methodology, results, limitations) kept separate.

## Where these tools still get it wrong

Every summarizer we tried occasionally flattened a hedge the authors were careful about — turning "suggests a possible link" into "proves a link." If you're citing the summary in your own work, always check the flagged sentence against the original paragraph.

## How to pick

If you only summarize a paper once in a while, a free tier is enough. If this is a weekly habit for a thesis or a lit review, the time saved from batch upload and consistent formatting is worth a paid plan.
