AI makes it easy to produce a clean summary of any topic. The risk for students is that a clean summary feels like learning without being learning.

## What the research says works

A widely cited review of learning techniques (Dunlosky et al., 2013) ranked common study methods by evidence of effectiveness. Two techniques stood out as high-utility:

- **Practice testing** (self-quizzing, flashcards, past papers) — strong evidence across subjects and ages.
- **Distributed practice** (spacing study over time) — strong evidence, especially for long-term retention.

Several popular methods ranked low-utility, including highlighting, underlining, and **rereading**. These feel productive but produce weak retention.

## Where AI genuinely helps

- Generating practice questions and flashcards from your notes or a textbook chapter.
- Producing varied phrasings of the same concept, which strengthens retrieval.
- Scheduling spaced review (e.g., an Anki-style deck populated by AI).

## Where AI hurts

- Producing a polished summary that you read passively. This is rereading with extra steps.
- Replacing self-testing with "explain it to me" chat sessions that never check whether you can recall the answer.
- Generating answers to practice questions before you have attempted them.

## A practical study loop

1. Read the source once.
2. Close it and write down what you remember.
3. Ask AI to generate 10 questions from the source.
4. Answer without looking, then check.
5. Revisit the missed items after a day, then a week.
